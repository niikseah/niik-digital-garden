Buyer Self-Collection Expansion

Competitor benchmarking and user journey mapping to grow self-collection coverage from 30% to 40%, plus end-to-end UAT so the supply-chain logic held up before deployment.

Context

Self-collection lets buyers pick up an order from a partner point instead of waiting on a home delivery — cheaper for the platform, faster for the buyer, and far less likely to fail on the last mile. Coverage sat at 30% of eligible orders, which meant most buyers never saw the option even where a collection point was a few minutes away.

The problem

The gap wasn't demand, it was reach and clarity: not enough eligible orders were being routed to collection points, and the ones that were didn't always make the trade-off obvious at checkout. Widening coverage also meant touching supply-chain logic that other flows depended on.

What I did

  1. 01

    Benchmarked the competition

    Compared how regional marketplaces surface, price, and time self-collection, and pulled out the patterns worth copying versus the ones that only work at a different scale.

  2. 02

    Mapped the buyer journey end to end

    Traced the path from checkout through collection-point handover to pickup, marking every point where a buyer drops out or an order silently falls back to home delivery.

  3. 03

    Turned findings into scoped requirements

    Translated the journey gaps into changes the supply-chain and front-end teams could actually pick up, sized against the coverage target.

  4. 04

    Ran end-to-end UAT before deployment

    Wrote and executed test cases across the order lifecycle so the routing logic held under real conditions instead of only in the happy path.
