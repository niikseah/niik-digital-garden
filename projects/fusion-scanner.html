Fusion Scanner

A browser extension that makes dense text readable for people it currently shuts out — multilingual readers, and anyone whose attention the modern web has fragmented.

Role
UX Researcher, UI Designer
Timeline

Team

Context

For many people, reading in your native language is a privilege comfortable enough to be invisible. For others it's a daily obstacle. Reading is still one of the primary ways we learn about the world, and several forces make it harder than it needs to be. Done in a group of four for CS3240: Interaction Design at NUS, through to the first redesign of the hi-fi.

The problem

Three things compound. The digital revolution expanded access to knowledge and then buried readers in it, so skimming replaced deep reading. The content itself carries unfamiliar vocabulary, culturally-embedded references, and domain jargon. And the tools meant to help don't: ChatGPT has no specialised interface for editing output through annotations, and Google Lens can't summarise at all.

Who it's for

Primary users are multilingual readers working through texts in a non-native language — international students, language learners, immigrants and new citizens.

A secondary group benefits indirectly: academics speeding through research papers, language tutors adapting the tool for teaching, frequent digital-media users, and people diagnosed with ADHD.

Three key tasks

  • Summarise the main points of a paragraph, quickly and easily
  • Define unfamiliar terms and industry jargon in place, with the system highlighting and clarifying on request
  • Import and export texts in common formats, and write and save annotations against them

Design process

  1. 01

    Contextual inquiries

    Findings were presented individually in a Sequence Work Model and a Cultural Work Model, then consolidated into a group affinity diagram.

  2. 02

    Key discoveries

    People sharing the same label face genuinely different reading experiences, so the nuances within a problem matter as much as the problem. Readers who seem fluent still benefit. And students have little control over what they're assigned to read or how long they get — which is where a tool can help most.

  3. 03

    Personas and scenarios

    Three personas were developed, including Leon Schmidt, a non-native international undergrad who reads carefully and slowly and consequently has nothing to contribute when the in-class discussion starts.

  4. 04

    Wireframes → prototype → evaluation

    Balsamiq wireframes, an interactive Figma prototype, then usability tests with target users and a heuristic evaluation feeding a documented redesign.

What the redesign changed

  • Clearer representation of the “From Highlighted” function
  • Asking for feedback before regenerating a summary
  • Separate regeneration controls for different portions of the summary
  • Warning pop-ups before high-risk actions
  • Better error and success messages
  • Improved help and documentation
