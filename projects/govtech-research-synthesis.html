Cross-Agency Research Synthesis

Distilled 450+ responses from a 6,000-participant initiative into requirements that defined MVP scope for 70% of target users, across seven organisations with competing definitions of the problem.

Context

A 6,000-participant initiative spanning seven organisations produced a large pile of qualitative feedback and no shared definition of the problem. Each agency arrived with its own framing, its own priority users, and its own idea of what a first release should contain.

The problem

Raw volume was the easy part. The hard part was that seven stakeholders each had a defensible but different answer to “who is this for first?” — and without a single answer, MVP scope would have expanded until it shipped nothing well.

What I did

  1. 01

    Coded 450+ responses into themes

    Worked through the response set and clustered it into recurring needs rather than agency-specific wording, so the same underlying problem stopped appearing as three different requests.

  2. 02

    Weighted themes by reach, not volume

    Ranked themes by how many of the target users they actually affected, which is what let a smaller scope still cover the majority.

  3. 03

    Reconciled seven competing framings

    Ran the themes back past each organisation and pinned down where the disagreement was real versus where it was vocabulary.

  4. 04

    Wrote requirements the build team could use

    Converted the agreed themes into requirements with enough specificity to scope, estimate, and cut.
