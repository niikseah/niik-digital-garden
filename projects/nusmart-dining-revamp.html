NUSmart Dining Revamp

A redesign of NUS's canteen pre-ordering app, focused on the one pain point that made students stop trusting it: nobody could tell how long they'd actually be waiting.

Role
UI Designer, UX Researcher
Timeline

Team

Tools

Context

NUSmart Dining lets students pre-order and pre-pay for meals in NUS canteens, saving both the student and the stall owner the queue. It's a good idea that students had stopped relying on. Done in a group of four for NM4259: Mobile Interaction Design, taken through to the first lo-fi design.

The problem

Research surfaced three real pain points — unreliable order tracking, no clear sense of wait time across stalls, and near-zero customisation for stalls like economical rice. The project scope allowed one. We took wait-time clarity, because it was the one that sent students to physically check on their orders anyway, defeating the whole point of the app.

Design process

  1. 01

    Empathise — three research methods

    Contextual inquiries with 2 people, interviews with 2 people, and a survey of 23, all aimed at understanding the NUS student's journey through the app.

  2. 02

    Define — empathy and affinity maps

    Findings were visualised in empathy maps and organised into themes by affinity mapping, then narrowed into a persona (Zach) and his user journey.

  3. 03

    Frame — How Might We

    Three HMWs came out of the journey: better-informed stall choice, more accurate displayed wait times, and seamless order tracking across classroom, walk, and canteen.

  4. 04

    Ideate and prototype

    Crazy 8's, a storyboard of the proposed scenario, a paper prototype, then lo-fi wireframes in Miro — plus an estimate of the cost of the work.

The scenario, before and after

Before: Zach picks a stall showing “3 orders waiting”, arrives to a queue of ten, watches his order sit at “processing” for fifteen minutes, and finds out the stall is prioritising the people physically queuing. He eats in a rush, arrives five minutes late to class, and skips lunch entirely the next day.

After: stalls show an estimated waiting time, colour-coded so the list can be scanned at a glance. Once he orders, the app shows how many orders are ahead of him and how long his food should take. A live notification tracks progress while he talks to a friend in the canteen, and vibrates when the food is ready. Afterwards, the app asks how long he actually waited — which is what keeps the estimates honest.

Overarching issues found

  • Uncertainty about waiting time, driven by no transparency into order progress
  • Inconsistent, unreliable information that depended entirely on stallholders updating it
  • Stalls listed as “closed” on the app while taking orders in person
  • Difficulty customising orders
