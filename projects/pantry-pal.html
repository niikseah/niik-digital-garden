Pantry Pal

A mobile app that tackles food waste by simplifying pantry management and meal planning — designed for 18–24 year olds, and aimed at the point of purchase rather than the point of disposal.

Role
UI Designer, UX Researcher
Timeline
1 month · Oct — Nov 2024
Team
Solo — NM4259 Mobile Interaction Design, NUS
Tools
Figma
  • User research
  • Empathy mapping
  • Prototyping
  • Figma

Context

Food waste is one of the five largest waste streams in Singapore. The apps that exist to fight it — food-sharing platforms like Olio, surplus-purchasing platforms like Treatsure — all act after the waste has been created. This project was done solo for NM4259: Mobile Interaction Design at NUS, taken through to the first hi-fi design.

The problem

Existing solutions treat the symptom. None of them touch the root cause: unsustainable consumer habits at the point of purchase. Without intervention there, the problem keeps escalating no matter how good the redistribution layer gets.

Target audience

Youths aged 18 to 24 — identified as a key demographic generating food waste. They need an intuitive, engaging solution that helps them plan smarter and builds habits that outlast the novelty of a new app.

Design process

  1. 01

    Barriers to action

    Two semi-structured interviews with the target audience surfaced why Singaporean youths disengage from waste-reducing practices. Four themes emerged: awareness of the environmental impact, a lack of immediate consequences, convenience winning over sustainability, and the sheer tediousness of waste-reducing practices.

  2. 02

    Empathy map and persona

    Interview findings were built into an empathy map and a persona to hold the interviewees' perspectives steady through the rest of the process.

  3. 03

    Existing vs. proposed user flow

    The existing flow follows John Tan through impulse buys, a duplicate frozen pizza his parents already bought, a forgotten ingredient, and finally expired food. The proposed flow replaces each of those failures with a specific feature — meal planning, a shared family list, real-time cart tracking, storage guidance, and expiry management.

  4. 04

    Lo-fi → testing → mid-fi

    The lo-fi prototype was tested across eight areas of the app, and the findings drove the mid-fi revision.

Key features

  • Meal Planner — plan meals, generate accurate shopping lists, avoid impulse buying
  • Expiry Date Tracker — timely reminders plus recipe suggestions based on what's expiring
  • Family Integration — shared pantry and grocery list, so nobody buys the pizza twice
  • Ease of Input — barcode scanning, voice input, and a grocery database
  • Proper Storage Methods — groceries auto-categorised by how they should be stored
  • Diet Tracker — calorie tracking layered onto pantry management, as an optional add-on

What testing changed

Two users read the “Cancel” button in the text-input field as “clear”, so it was redesigned as “Back”. A participant asked for the “Pantry” button to sit in the centre of the nav bar; it moved, because it matches user convention, while “Profile” stayed on the right as the least-accessed tab.

A/B testing settled two open questions: participants overwhelmingly preferred a grid view for expired groceries, because they identify food by its packaging, and preferred the whole week visible in the Meal Planner, because that matches weekly grocery habits. For the “XX days left” expiry notice, the smaller bottom-left placement won — noticeable but unobtrusive.

Watching two users try to find recipes from expiring ingredients on the Pantry page led to a new Ingredients page, where recipes, nutritional value, storage, and quantity all live together.

Design decisions

Receipt scanning was suggested and rejected: receipts carry no expiry dates and abbreviate item names past recognition. Manual expiry entry stayed, which has the side effect of making the shopper think about shelf life at the moment they're handling the food.

The Diet Tracker moved to the Profile page. Pantry Pal is a pantry manager first; diet tracking is secondary and optional, activated after account creation rather than pushed on everyone.

The colour scheme leans green to signal the sustainability goal, with calm tones chosen for contrast and accessibility.
