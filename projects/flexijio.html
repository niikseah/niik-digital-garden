FlexiJio

A desktop video-conferencing platform built specifically for fitness — so friends at different fitness levels can take the same class together, each doing a version of the exercise that suits them.

Role
UX Researcher, UI Designer
Timeline
3 months · Feb — Apr 2024
Team
Group of 4 — NM3243 User Experience Design, NUS
Tools
Miro
  • User research
  • Personas
  • Usability heuristics
  • Miro

Context

Remote work pushed demand for at-home fitness up, and the available options all fall short in a different direction. Online personal-training software like EverFit and iFit sells individual classes without long-term accountability. Facebook Live and Zoom were never designed for fitness and miss the features that would matter. YouTube workouts demand self-discipline and give no personalised guidance. Done in a group of four for NM3243: User Experience Design at NUS, through to the first redesign of the lo-fi.

The problem

People with busy lifestyles need something that serves both the short-term need — get moving today — and the long-term one — still be doing this in six months. Nothing on the market held both ends at once.

Target user group

Office workers aged 18 to 63: the bulk of the potential user base. People working remotely who want to exercise at home, but with other people, and with professional guidance.

Design process

  1. 01

    Research

    Semi-structured interviews plus indirect observation through written diary logs — the interviews for individual needs, the diaries for behavioural patterns over time.

  2. 02

    Key discoveries

    Users want virtual fitness to feel as personal and responsive as in-person training. And while motivation can come from within, external community support is what sustains engagement — people reported a greater sense of achievement when others were involved.

  3. 03

    Requirements definition

    Fitness competency turned out to be the influential variable, so two personas were built around it: Melina Ching, 50, fitness-competent, frustrated that exercising with less-fit friends means compromising intensity; and Ryan Tan, 35, fitness-incompetent, trying to start.

  4. 04

    Concept to prototype

    Metaphors, storyboarding, wireframes, a lo-fi prototype, user testing, a documented redesign separating incorporated from unincorporated feedback, then a final prototype checked against usability heuristics.

What sets it apart

  • Community-driven engagement — accountability that survives past week two
  • Gamification — rewards, leaderboards, and progress tracking to make consistency fun
  • Personalised exercises — expert and simplified variants of the same lesson, so friends at different levels take the same class together

Scope, stated honestly

FlexiJio is for people who want to exercise with others from home. It does not serve users who prefer to train alone, or whose schedules don't line up with class times. It's a complementary option alongside gyms and in-person classes, not a replacement for them.

Reflection

The group's understanding of the problem space clarified over time, driven by user data and the professor's comments. The qualitative methods gave depth, but more data is needed for a consensus that genuinely reflects the target users — and we collected from people we know, which likely made the feedback less critical than it should have been.

The first lo-fi prototype is the one I had reservations about: we focused too much on aesthetics and not enough on interactions, so the testing findings came back about distracting visuals instead of how the thing actually works. With another iteration I'd extend the design to users with mobility issues, who could attend the same class as their friends precisely because the system already shows different exercises to different people.

What I'm proudest of is that varying-difficulty feature — it's FlexiJio's real selling point — and that the design holds both goals at once: gamification to get people started, community to keep them going.
